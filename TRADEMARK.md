# Whity Trademark Policy

**Version 1.0**

The Whity Core *software* is free. The Whity *name* is not, and this document
explains the difference.

---

## Why a free project has a trademark policy

Under the AGPL you may run, modify, fork, sell and redistribute Whity Core
without asking anyone. That is a deliberate grant and this policy does not
narrow it by a single line.

Copyright and trademark protect different things. Copyright covers the code;
trademark covers the **name people rely on to know what they are getting**. If
anyone could ship anything called "Whity", then a user with a broken,
abandoned or hostile fork would come to us for support, and the name would stop
carrying information. Every major copyleft project — Linux, Firefox, WordPress,
Odoo, GitLab, Grafana — holds its trademark for this reason while giving the
code away.

This is also, honestly, where the commercial leverage sits. The licence is
generous *because* the name is protected. One makes the other affordable.

## What you may do, without asking

- **Use the software.** Run, modify, deploy and sell services around Whity
  Core, on the AGPL's terms.
- **Say what is true.** "Built on Whity", "compatible with Whity", "Whity
  hosting", "we implement Whity" — nominative use is fine and always will be.
  You do not need permission to state a fact.
- **Fork it.** Under a different name.
- **Write about it.** Tutorials, courses, books, talks, reviews — favourable or
  not.
- **Publish plugins** named for what they do: "Invoicing for Whity", "Whity
  Inventory Connector". A plugin describing its compatibility is nominative use.

## What needs permission

- **Naming a product or company Whity**, or a name likely to be confused with
  it — including domains, package names and app-store listings that suggest
  they are the official project.
- **Distributing modified Whity Core under the name "Whity."** Fork freely;
  rename when you do. This is the one that matters most: a user must be able to
  trust that "Whity" means this project's code.
- **Using the Whity logo or wordmark** as your own branding, or in a way that
  implies endorsement, partnership or certification.
- **Claiming to be official** — "official Whity partner", "Whity certified",
  "Whity Enterprise" — unless there is an agreement saying so.

## Never

- Registering "Whity", or a confusingly similar mark, as a trademark or domain
  in any jurisdiction.
- Using the name in a way that suggests the project endorses your product,
  service or opinion when it does not.

---

## Removing the branding

Whity Core has a branding subsystem, and you may replace the name, logo and
theme in your own deployment. That is supported, and for white-label use it is
the correct path.

If you white-label, you must not present the result as "Whity", and you must
not present Whity as the source of your product's warranties or support.
